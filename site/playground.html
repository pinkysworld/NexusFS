What to look for

  • The state root, not the head. The head carries per-device provenance, so two replicas holding identical data still have different heads. The state root is a pure function of the applied operations — it is what convergence actually means here.
  • Sync moves operations, then content. Each transfer carries the oplog plus the blobs it references. Blob hashes are verified on arrival before anything is stored.
  • Conflicts do not lose data. Create /shared on both replicas while partitioned, then sync. Both links survive; the one with the lower dot keeps the plain name and the other is renamed deterministically, identically on both sides.
  • Order does not matter. Sync in either direction, in any order, as many times as you like. Re-applying an operation is a no-op, and operations that arrive before their parent are parked and retried.

Caveats

This is the local core, not the network stack

Replication here is a button, not a protocol: the page hands one replica's oplog and blobs to the other in-process. The QUIC transport, peer handshake and Have/Want negotiation are still unimplemented — that is milestone M3. Encryption at rest and proof enforcement are not wired in either. State lives in memory and is gone on reload.
